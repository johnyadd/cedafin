A Ghanaian business owner looking for credit is told an interest rate. That
rate is not what the loan costs, and the gap between the two is larger than
most borrowers expect.

Here is what the regulator's own figures show, and how to use them.

## The one figure to ask for

**Ask for the APR, not the interest rate.**

The interest rate is one component. The annual percentage rate includes
everything else — arrangement fees, commitment fees, processing charges,
insurance — expressed as a single annual figure.

Bank of Ghana requires every bank to calculate and report it. So it exists, it
is already worked out, and any bank can quote it. A bank that will only give
you an interest rate is giving you the smaller half of the number.

**How much smaller?** Bank of Africa's one-year SME loan is reported at a
minimum of 13.70% and a maximum of 23.42%. Nearly ten percentage points of
that range is fees.

## What the market actually charges

Twenty-two banks report. For a one-year business loan in May 2026:

| Bank | APR |
|---|---|
| **Standard Chartered** | **11.03%** |
| Ecobank Ghana | 13.01% |
| First Atlantic Bank | 13.51% |
| Bank of Africa | 13.70% |
| … | |
| GCB Bank | 27.54% |
| **Guaranty Trust Bank** | **33.58%** |

The cheapest and the dearest are **22.55 percentage points apart** — for the
same product, in the same market, in the same month.

On GH₵100,000 over one year that is roughly **GH₵22,550**. More than the entire
cost of borrowing from the cheapest lender.

## Why the range within a single bank matters as much

Every bank reports a minimum and a maximum, and the spread is often wide. Bank
of Africa's runs from 13.70% to 23.42%.

Both figures are real. Some borrowers get the lower one — usually those with
security, a trading history with the bank, or a stronger balance sheet.

**So the question to ask is not just "what is your APR" but "where in your
range would I fall, and why".** A bank reporting a ten-point spread knows the
answer before it lends. Getting it before you apply tells you whether you are
being offered the advertised rate or the other end of it.

## What almost no Ghanaian bank publishes

We checked the websites of every bank we track. **Three mention a rate.** The
rest publish product descriptions, eligibility criteria and application forms
with no figure at all.

That is not a regulatory failure — Bank of Ghana collects the data and
publishes it. It is a gap between the regulator's publication, which few
borrowers read, and the bank's own material, which they all do.

Also usually missing: the arrangement fee as a separate figure, whether
security is required, how long a decision takes, and what happens if you repay
early.

## Before you apply

**Compare on APR, across at least four banks.** The spread is too wide to take
the first offer. Our comparison covers twenty-two.

**Ask for the fee breakdown in writing.** Arrangement, commitment, processing,
insurance — each as a cedi amount, not a percentage of something unspecified.

**Ask what security is needed**, and whether the rate changes without it.

**Ask how long a decision takes.** For working capital this often matters more
than half a point on the rate.

**And check the term.** A three-year loan at a lower rate can cost more in
total than one year at a higher one. Our comparison covers one, three and five
years separately for that reason.

## What this cannot tell you

**Whether you should borrow at all.** That is a judgement about your business,
and we do not make it.

**What you personally will be offered.** The figures here are what banks
reported to the regulator across their whole book. Your rate depends on your
circumstances.

**And what has changed since May 2026.** APR reports are published
periodically, and rates move. The date on every figure we publish is the date
it was true.

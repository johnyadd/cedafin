Every Ghanaian bank reports two numbers to Bank of Ghana. The lending rate it
advertises, and the annual percentage rate a borrower actually pays once fees
are counted.

The distance between them is the fee load. We have four of these reports,
spanning twenty months, and the direction is good news — with a qualification
that swallows most of it.

## The gap is narrowing

Average distance between advertised rate and all-in APR, across every bank
reporting a one-year facility:

| | Household | SME | Corporate |
|---|---|---|---|
| September 2024 | 6.13pp | 4.21pp | 2.92pp |
| March 2025 | 4.83pp | 4.95pp | 2.44pp |
| June 2025 | 5.49pp | 4.22pp | 2.65pp |
| **May 2026** | **4.36pp** | **3.54pp** | **1.48pp** |
| Change | **−29%** | **−16%** | **−49%** |

Fees are a smaller part of the cost of borrowing than they were. On every
category, for every kind of customer.

## And the smallest borrowers have gained least

Corporate fee load has halved. Household has fallen by not quite a third.
**Small business has fallen by a sixth.**

Which means the disparity has grown rather than shrunk. In September 2024 a
household borrower carried **2.1 times** the fee load of a corporate one. By
May 2026 that had become **2.9 times**.

The direction is right for everybody and the distance between them is widening.

## Why this matters more than the headline rate

Interest rates are visible. A bank advertises them, a borrower compares them,
and news coverage reports when they move.

**Fees are not.** No Ghanaian bank publishes a breakdown of what its
arrangement, processing and monitoring charges come to. The only reason these
figures exist at all is that Bank of Ghana requires the calculation and
publishes the result.

So a small business owner watching lending rates fall over the past two years
would have had no way of knowing that their share of the improvement was a
third of what a large corporate got.

## What this does not establish

**Four observations are not a trend.** These are September 2024, March 2025,
June 2025 and May 2026 — unevenly spaced, and the middle two move in different
directions. A monthly series would show whether this is a steady decline or
noise around one.

**The banks reporting are not identical each time.** Between nineteen and
twenty-two institutions appear in each category and period. Some of the
movement could be composition rather than behaviour.

**And these are averages across each bank's whole book**, not quotes. Bank of
Ghana says plainly that a given borrower may be offered something different
after assessment.

## Why we could only find four reports

Bank of Ghana publishes this monthly. The current notice is on their site.

**The previous ones are not.** We checked eight earlier months and found no
published notice for any of them — the analysis above is possible only because
we had saved the PDFs as they appeared.

A monthly publication with no accessible archive can tell a borrower what
credit costs today and nothing about whether it is getting better or worse. We
have written to Bank of Ghana to ask whether the older returns are available
somewhere we have not found, and will publish what they say.

Meanwhile we are keeping every report as it publishes. In a year this table
will have twelve more rows.

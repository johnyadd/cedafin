To buy a share on the Ghana Stock Exchange you must go through a licensed
dealing member. There are twenty-four of them. In August 2026 we visited every
one of their websites to find out what they charge.

Not one publishes a commission rate.

One publishes a minimum to open an account. Three describe their rates as
"competitive" without giving a figure. Six did not respond at all.

## What that means in practice

Someone in Accra with GH₵5,000, or a Ghanaian in London wanting exposure to
companies back home, cannot find out the price of a trade before committing to
a firm. They must choose a broker, open an account, hand over identity
documents — and only then learn what it costs.

For comparison, an international platform quotes 0.75% for Ghanaian shares. That
figure is public. What a member firm in Accra charges is not.

The Securities and Exchange Commission publishes where each broker is, its
telephone number, its email and its licence status. It does not require them to
publish what they charge, and none volunteers it.

Sarpong Capital Markets is marked because the exchange's reports show it
appearing in seven monthly reports and trading nothing in any of them.

## Who actually does the business

The exchange publishes each member's share of value traded every month. Across
the fifteen reports we hold, from February 2025 to July 2026:

| Broker | Average share | Range |
|---|---|---|
| **IC Securities** | **52.70%** | **19.97–78.82%** |
| SBG Securities | 16.59% | 0.27–74.13% |
| Databank Brokerage | 8.36% | 2.20–27.64% |
| Blackstar Brokerage | 5.47% | 2.19–11.65% |
| Constant Capital | 3.80% | 0.05–27.51% |

The top three handle about 78% of the value traded between them.

But read the range rather than the average. IC Securities' share, month by
month, went 68%, 49%, 67%, 42%, 20%, 66%, 45%, 33%, 23%, 57%, 77%, 50%, 64%,
51%, 79%.

That is a fifty-nine point swing with no direction. It is not a firm
consolidating power — it means a handful of large trades decide who leads in any
given month, which is a fact about how thin the exchange is rather than about
any broker's position.

## Value and volume point different ways

There is one signal in the data that gets closer to what an investor actually
wants to know: whether a firm wants small business.

In July 2026, IC Securities handled 78.82% of the value traded but 62.16% of the
volume — fewer, larger trades. Databank handled 3.32% of value and 7.77% of
volume: more trades, smaller ones.

Nobody publishes whether they will take a GH₵5,000 client. This is as close as
the public data comes, and it points the opposite way to the value ranking.

## One thing worth knowing before you look

Ghanaian brokers do not specialise. The exchange's monthly reports name no
market makers and no sponsoring broker for any listing, so any of the
twenty-four can buy any listed share, and the NewGold ETF.

There is no need to find the right firm for a particular company. What differs
between them is cost, whether they want retail business, and how easily an
account can be opened — and only the last of those is published by anyone.

## We have asked

Every one of the twenty-four has been sent the same questions: what you charge
to buy and sell, any minimum to open an account, what you deal in, whether you
take retail clients, and how an account is opened.

Anything that comes back will be published beside the firm's name, cited and
dated, at no cost to them. So will a refusal — "declined to provide" beside a
blank field is itself something an investor can use.

The first Ghanaian broker to publish a rate will be the only one whose costs
anybody can check before committing.

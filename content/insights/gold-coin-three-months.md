Between 1 June and 28 August 2026 the price of gold barely moved. It opened
the period at $4,545.95 an ounce and closed it at $4,568.95 — up half a
percent.

Over those same three months a one-ounce Ghana Gold Coin fell from
GH₵55,181.16 to GH₵53,013.65. Down 3.93%.

Nothing went wrong. The coin tracked the metal precisely. What changed was the
cedi, which strengthened from 11.735 to the dollar to 11.215 — a gain of 4.4%.

## The arithmetic, in full

A gold coin priced in cedis is the dollar gold price multiplied by the
exchange rate. Both numbers are printed on the same Bank of Ghana circular
every working day, which is what makes this checkable.

At the start of June: $4,545.95 × 11.735 = GH₵53,347 of metal.
At the end of August: $4,568.95 × 11.215 = GH₵51,240.

A fall of 3.95%, against the coin's 3.93%. The two agree, because the coin is
the metal and the coin's price is the metal's price converted.

## Which is the opposite of what a hedge is supposed to do

Gold is recommended to Ghanaian savers as protection. The thing most of them
want protecting from is the cedi losing value — and over this period the cedi
did the opposite.

**That is not a fault in the coin.** A currency hedge pays when the currency
falls and costs you when it rises. This quarter it rose. A holder who bought
for protection got the mechanism they paid for, working in the direction that
does not help.

The same arithmetic runs the other way when the cedi weakens, which it has
done far more often than not. Somebody deciding today should understand that
they are taking a position on the exchange rate, not stepping outside it.

## The smallest coin costs the most

Bank of Ghana issues three sizes, and the premium over the metal in them is
not the same:

| | Premium over metal | Price, 28 Aug 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| 1 oz | 3.58% | GH₵53,014 |
| ½ oz | 4.83% | GH₵26,858 |
| ¼ oz | 7.75% | GH₵13,803 |

The quarter-ounce carries more than twice the premium of the full ounce. It is
the coin a smaller saver can afford, and it is the one that costs most for the
gold inside it.

That is ordinary for minted coins — the cost of striking and distributing a
coin does not fall in proportion to its weight — but it is rarely stated where
a buyer will see it before paying.

## After inflation

Annualised from this three-month window, and measured against Ghanaian
inflation, the one-ounce coin returns **−18.99%** a year. The half-ounce
−18.91%, the quarter-ounce −18.76%.

Those are annualised figures from a single quarter, not a loss of 19% over
three months. A quarter is a short window to annualise from and the number
should be read as a rate rather than an outcome. It is shown because the
alternative — a nominal figure with no inflation beside it — is half a number.

## One more thing

Bank of Ghana published a Ghana Gold Coin pricing circular every working day
from launch. Their notice list shows the most recent dated **2 September
2026**. Nothing has appeared since.

They may resume. But a daily price that stops without notice is worth
recording, and a saver holding the coin has no published price to value it
against.

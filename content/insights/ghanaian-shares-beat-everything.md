Between February 2025 and July 2026, the Ghana Stock Exchange Composite Index
rose from 5,659.76 to 15,434.87. That is 172.7% in seventeen months.

Nothing else available to a Ghanaian investor came close.

## What each asset actually did

| | Return | Period |
|---|---|---|
| **GSE Composite Index** | **+172.7%** | **Feb 2025 – Jul 2026** |
| Stanbic Income Fund Trust | +38.80% | 1 year |
| First Atlantic Income Fund | +34.73% | 1 year |
| 364-day Treasury bill | 11.59% | current rate |
| 91-day Treasury bill | 5.08% | current rate |
| Ghana Gold Coin, 1 oz | −3.93% | Jun–Aug, in cedis |

The periods differ and the risks differ enormously — a single share is not a
diversified fund, and none of this is a forecast. But an investor deciding
where to put money in Ghana over that period had one answer that beat the rest
by a distance.

## And it happened on rising, not falling, trade

This is the part worth dwelling on, because the obvious suspicion about a rise
that size is that it happened on thin trading — prices moving because nobody
was selling rather than because people were buying.

The opposite is true.

| | Feb 2025 | Jul 2026 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Composite index | 5,659.76 | 15,434.87 | +172.7% |
| **Shares traded** | **23.3m** | **100.4m** | **+331.2%** |
| Value traded | GH₵162.6m | GH₵683.5m | +320.3% |
| Market capitalisation | GH₵127.8bn | GH₵292.5bn | +128.8% |

Volume quadrupled. Value traded more than quadrupled. More people bought more
shares more often, and prices rose with them — which is the healthy version of
a bull market rather than the fragile one.

A word of caution about a figure that circulates. The July 2026 report shows
volume down 71.98% year on year, which looks alarming and is real — but it
compares one month against an exceptional July 2025. Seventeen months of data
say the opposite, and a series beats a snapshot.

## Which shares

Thirty-nine companies are listed. The moves within them are far wider than the
index suggests:

| Share | Sector | Price move |
|---|---|---|
| **Clydestone** | **Technology** | **+15,733%** |
| Intravenous Infusions | Manufacturing | +1,580% |
| SIC Insurance | Insurance | +1,451% |
| Cocoa Processing | Food and beverage | +750% |
| Republic Bank | Finance | +600% |
| GCB Bank | Finance | +564% |

Clydestone went from 3 pesewas to GH₵4.75. That is a genuine, steadily climbing
move across fifteen monthly readings, not a data error — and it is exactly the
kind of number that should make a reader cautious rather than excited. A share
priced at 3 pesewas is a company the market had nearly written off, and moves
like that go both ways.

Meanwhile several shares did nothing at all, and two fell.

## What this page cannot tell you

**What it costs to buy any of it.** Brokerage, charged by whichever licensed
dealing member you use, and [not one of the twenty-four publishes a
rate](/brokers).

**Dividends — and a correction.** The exchange's monthly report defines
dividend yield in its glossary and publishes it for no company, so every figure
above is a price move only and understates what a holder received.

An earlier version of this piece implied the data does not exist. It does, just
not from the exchange. Black Star Advisors publishes a Ghana Equity Total Return
Index accounting for both capital gains and dividends, and per-company dividend
yields are available from third-party data providers. We do not yet hold either
in a form we can date and source, which is why the figures above remain price
moves — but "the exchange does not publish it" is not the same as
"nobody does".

**Anything about the businesses.** Earnings, debt, management, sector
conditions. A share is a claim on a company, and a price chart says nothing
about whether the company is sound.

**And whether any of it repeats.** Ghanaian Treasury bills paid over 20% in
early 2025 and 5.08% in August 2026. Money that left deposits looking for
return had to go somewhere, and some of it went here. That is a rate cycle, and
cycles turn.

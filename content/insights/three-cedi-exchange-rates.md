Every report on the cedi quotes one number. On 3 September 2026 Bank of Ghana
published the dollar at GH₵11.3343 and the pound at GH₵15.3073 on the interbank
market.

That is the wholesale price between banks. Nobody transacts at it, but it is
the floor everything else is measured against — and how far your own rate sits
from it depends entirely on what you are doing.

## 1. Sending money home

What the recipient gets per unit sent. Higher is better.

| Provider | Per US$1 | vs interbank | Per £1 | vs interbank |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wise | 11.33 | −0.0% | 15.29 | −0.1% |
| LemFi | 11.30 | −0.3% | 15.32 | +0.1% |
| Sendwave | 11.30 | −0.3% | 15.32 | +0.1% |
| WorldRemit | 11.30 | −0.3% | 15.32 | +0.1% |
| TapTap Send | 11.30 | −0.3% | 15.32 | +0.1% |
| Remitly | 11.24 | −0.8% | 15.24 | −0.4% |
| **Western Union** | **10.20** | **−10.0%** | 15.32 | +0.1% |

**The received wisdom is out of date.** Transfer apps are giving essentially
the interbank rate on both currencies — several are fractionally above it. On
the rate alone, competition has compressed the margin to almost nothing.

**With one exception, and it is a large one.** Western Union was at GH₵10.20 to
the dollar, ten per cent below interbank and ten per cent below every other
provider on the list. On US$1,000 that is about GH₵1,100 less than the same
transfer through Wise. Their sterling rate was in line with everyone else, so
it is the dollar corridor specifically.

**Fees are separate.** A good rate with a flat fee can still be the expensive
option on a small transfer. Compare what actually arrives, not the rate alone.

## 2. Changing cash after you arrive

What a forex bureau in Accra gives you for notes in hand.

| Bureau | Per US$1 | vs interbank | Per £1 | vs interbank |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Aboki** | **11.75** | **+3.7%** | 15.50 | +1.3% |
| Dawula | 11.70 | +3.2% | 15.50 | +1.3% |
| City BDC | 11.70 | +3.2% | 15.30 | −0.0% |

**This is the surprise.** Changing dollar notes at a bureau gets you *more*
than sending the same money through an app — about 3.7% more at the best rate
we found, and 1.2% more on sterling.

That is not what most people expect, and it is worth understanding why: a
bureau is buying physical currency it can resell at a margin, while a transfer
app is moving a number. Different businesses, different economics.

**It is not free money.** Carrying cash means carrying the risk, and the
difference on a few hundred dollars will not cover a lost wallet. But if you
are travelling anyway and have notes, the bureau is not the rip-off it is
usually assumed to be.

## 3. Buying foreign currency as a Ghanaian

What you pay per unit — to travel, to import, or to pay a foreign website.
Lower is better, and both options are dear.

| How | Per US$1 | vs interbank | Per £1 | vs interbank |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Forex bureau, selling | 12.12 | +6.9% | 16.23 | +6.0% |
| **Card payment** | **12.15** | **+7.2%** | **16.44** | **+7.4%** |

**This is where the real cost sits.** A Ghanaian paying a foreign website with
a Visa or Mastercard is charged about 7% over the interbank rate before any
card fee. A bureau is fractionally cheaper and requires you to be standing in
one.

On a GH₵5,000 foreign purchase, the difference between the card rate and the
interbank rate is roughly GH₵340.

## The pattern

The same currency on the same day moved 7% depending on the channel — and
which channel is best depends on which direction you are going.

**Sending money in:** an app, and almost any of them except Western Union on
dollars.

**Arriving with cash:** a bureau, which beats the apps.

**Buying foreign currency:** both options cost about 7%, and there is no good
one.

## What to do with this

**Know the baseline.** Bank of Ghana publishes daily interbank rates for the
dollar, pound, euro, Swiss franc, Canadian and Australian dollars, krone, krona
and yen. Measure your quote against that, not against a competitor's
advertisement.

**Check the direction.** A rate that looks good for someone sending money is
not the same rate as the one for someone buying currency, and quoting one at
the other is how most confusion here starts.

**And check today.** These figures are 3 September 2026. Rates move daily —
CediRates publishes them for Ghanaian banks, bureaux, cards and transfer apps,
and Bank of Ghana publishes the interbank baseline. The method outlasts the
numbers.
